The following steps are used to extract curvature inducing stresses at material interfaces. Briefly in this assay, pressure is measured in a needle that creates bubbles with a pump in a sample of interest. 

1. First, the effective radius(radii), r, of the needle(s) are found using samples of known surface tensions, Y (e.g., ethanol) with the Laplace pressure, Pc = 2Y/r, at the moment of bubble bursting. 
[Note:  
	- the spherical assumption for Laplace's relationship holds better for smaller capillaries.
	- any air resistance in the needle is avoided by starting the pump before penetration and zeroing for that pressure. 
	- using the effective r may also calibrate the system for viscous resistances as well.
	- keep needle tip away from the boundaries and drive the stuck bubbles (if any) out of the way for cavitation consistency. 
]

2. The calibration flow rate and needle penetration depth are to be kept the same as the calibration while measuring the sample

3. The consecutive critical pressures (Pc) may differ significantly before stabilizing around a certain value. The Pressure-time data is to be recorded after this stage is reached.   
[Note: this stabilization is not the same as surface adsorption equilibration]

4. Repeat step 3 if multiple needles are to be used. 
[Note: 
	- the needle sizes need to be fairly different (e.g., 27Gg and 31Gg) if the measurements are to be treated as independent in the calculations. 
	- the flow rates, Q, are to be kept low for better adsorption equilibrations. 
	- when using multiple needles, Q should be selected such that Q1/Q2 ~ (r2/r1)^2 to keep the area expansion ratios (area hencky strains) close. This helps with decoupling the surface dilatational modulus (please refer 	  to the slides) by keeping dr/dt constant for the needles.  
]
